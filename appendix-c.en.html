C.1.3 Repeated Action Sequences Across Tasks

This analysis checks whether models repeat the same tool-call order across tasks. It retains only function names, such as retrieving a file and then sending an email; recipient and file identifiers are excluded. For task iii in archive rrr, denote its action-name sequence by:

ari=(name⁡(ari1),…,name⁡(ariTri)). \mathbf a_{ri} = \left( \operatorname{name}(a_{ri1}), \ldots, \operatorname{name}(a_{riT_{ri}}) \right). ari​=(name(ari1​),…,name(ariTri​​)).

Two sequences count as repeated when their function names and order are identical. To distinguish repetition across different task requirements, task pairs are split into same-type and different-type groups using the behavior types defined by the task rules.

Let Ir\mathcal I_rIr​ contain tasks with both a behavior-type label and a trajectory record, and let bib_ibi​ denote task iii's behavior type. Each pair is compared once; i<ji<ji<j avoids duplicate counting:

P≠(r)={(i,j):i,j∈Ir, i<j, bi≠bj}, \mathcal P_{\ne}^{(r)} = \left\{ (i,j):i,j\in\mathcal I_r,\ i<j,\ b_i\ne b_j \right\}, P=(r)​={(i,j):i,j∈Ir​, i<j, bi​=bj​},
P=(r)={(i,j):i,j∈Ir, i<j, bi=bj}. \mathcal P_{=}^{(r)} = \left\{ (i,j):i,j\in\mathcal I_r,\ i<j,\ b_i=b_j \right\}. P=(r)​={(i,j):i,j∈Ir​, i<j, bi​=bj​}.

For each group of task pairs, compute the proportion with identical action-name sequences:

Cbetween(r)=#{(i,j)∈P≠(r):ari=arj}∣P≠(r)∣, C_{\mathrm{between}}^{(r)} = \frac{ \#\left\{ (i,j)\in\mathcal P_{\ne}^{(r)}: \mathbf a_{ri}=\mathbf a_{rj} \right\} }{ \left|\mathcal P_{\ne}^{(r)}\right| }, Cbetween(r)​=​P=(r)​​#{(i,j)∈P=(r)​:ari​=arj​}​,
Cwithin(r)=#{(i,j)∈P=(r):ari=arj}∣P=(r)∣. C_{\mathrm{within}}^{(r)} = \frac{ \#\left\{ (i,j)\in\mathcal P_{=}^{(r)}: \mathbf a_{ri}=\mathbf a_{rj} \right\} }{ \left|\mathcal P_{=}^{(r)}\right| }. Cwithin(r)​=​P=(r)​​#{(i,j)∈P=(r)​:ari​=arj​}​.

C.2.1 Option Completion in Intermediate Tied Groups

We evaluate four models from early experiments on 50 prompts each, generating eight trajectories per prompt at temperature 1.0, or 400 trajectories per model. They come from two GiGPO runs with different seeds, the prompt-group baseline (askact_group_phi, k=2), and training that rewards visits only when the terminal-score threshold is met.

For prompt group jjj, define:

Δj=max⁡1≤i≤8Rji−min⁡1≤i≤8Rji. \Delta_j = \max_{1\le i\le 8}R_{ji} - \min_{1\le i\le 8}R_{ji}. Δj​=1≤i≤8max​Rji​−1≤i≤8min​Rji​.

The prompt group is tied when:

Δj<10−9. \Delta_j<10^{-9}. Δj​<10−9.

Returns here use env_reward from the saved outputs, excluding visit rewards. Once a group meets the range criterion above, its first trajectory’s return determines its category:

C.2.2 Prompt-Group Baseline Overlap and External-Table Coverage

Overlapping Trajectories in the Prompt-Group Baseline

The prompt-group baseline in B.2 combines an anchor mean with the overall prompt-group mean. Each group has eight trajectories; excluding the current trajectory leaves N=7N=7N=7. Let mmm count the remaining trajectories that visited the current anchor. This run uses k=2k=2k=2, giving the anchor mean a direct weight of:

wdirect=mm+k. w_{\mathrm{direct}}=\frac{m}{m+k}. wdirect​=m+km​.

The overall prompt-group mean also contains these mmm trajectories, with a share of m/Nm/Nm/N. The same anchor trajectories therefore contribute again through the overall mean. Their combined weight in the final baseline is:

weffective=mm+k+km+kmN=m(N+k)N(m+k). w_{\mathrm{effective}} =\frac{m}{m+k}+\frac{k}{m+k}\frac{m}{N} =\frac{m(N+k)}{N(m+k)}. weffective​=m+km​+m+kk​Nm​=N(m+k)m(N+k)​.

The diagnostic computes these weights for pairs of a trajectory and an anchor it visited. Mean direct weight is 0.739. Including the overlap inside the overall mean raises the mean effective weight to 0.951.

In approximately 75% of these pairs, all seven other trajectories visited the current anchor, so m=N=7m=N=7m=N=7. Then:

wdirect=79,weffective=1. w_{\mathrm{direct}}=\frac79, \qquad w_{\mathrm{effective}}=1. wdirect​=97​,weffective​=1.

Both means then come from exactly the same seven trajectories. Although the formula has two terms, their weighted combination is still the mean return of those seven trajectories. Overall, anchor-visiting trajectories carry an average of 95.1% of the baseline weight, leaving little weight for other trajectories through the overall prompt-group mean.

C.2.3 Probability Differences Between Sampling and Training

During 75 optimizer steps of a WebShop run, we compare token probabilities recorded by the sampling backend with probabilities recomputed by the training model. Both probabilities refer to the same sampled token in the same context. Appendix B.2 defines the token-level and sequence-level χ2\chi^2χ2 diagnostics.

This run disables actor.use_rollout_log_probs. The policy loss uses old-policy probabilities recomputed on the training side; sampling-side probabilities supply the diagnostics below.

The table compares steps 1–25 with steps 51–75. Correlation and mean absolute difference are computed over tokens within each step and then averaged over the 25-step window. Maximum absolute difference is first taken within each step, followed by the median of the 25 maxima.

Diagnostic Steps 1–25 Steps 51–75
Mean Pearson correlation between the two sets of probabilities 0.9770 0.9600
Mean absolute probability difference 0.00259 0.00193
Median per-step maximum absolute probability difference 0.595 0.486
Median sequence-level χ2\chi^2χ2 0.0561 0.0622

The mean absolute probability difference is about 0.002, but the median per-step maximum is close to 0.5. The average therefore conceals much larger differences on some tokens.

The sequence-level diagnostic multiplies token probability ratios along each sequence before computing the statistic. Of the 75 steps, 66 have positive token-level χ2\chi^2χ2 and a finite ratio below. Across these steps:

median⁡(χsequence2χtoken2)=39.5. \operatorname{median}\left( \frac{\chi^2_{\mathrm{sequence}}}{\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}} \right)=39.5. median(χtoken2​χsequence2​​)=39.5.

In the later window, mean absolute probability difference is smaller, while median sequence-level χ2\chi^2χ2 rises from 0.0561 to 0.0622. Checking agreement between the two backends therefore requires examining both large differences on individual tokens and accumulated differences across complete sequences.
