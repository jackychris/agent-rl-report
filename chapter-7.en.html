7. Discussion and Conclusion

7.1. Diagnostic Order Across Evaluation, Sampling, and Updates

Combining the Ask-or-Act and WebShop diagnostics yields a six-step audit whenever terminal training scores rise or plateau (Table 7.1). Checking evaluator denominators, condition-shift order, and candidate rollout diversity before modifying rewards prevents attributing sampling collapse or normalization cancellation to reward misspecification.

Table 7.1: Six-step diagnostic checklist spanning evaluator coverage, condition-shift behavior, rollout diversity, reward ordering, normalized advantage, and parameter updates.

Step Question Primary Evidence Source Decision Branch
1. Evaluator coverage Are call order, handle binding, and omitted writes directly measured? Deterministic replay fields and call-order checks (BpreB_{\mathrm{pre}}Bpre​, failed_to_act). Separate completion from ordering and compliance denominators.
2. Condition-shift split Where do (A, B) pairs diverge when tool locations or rules shift? Paired trajectory inspection (§4.1, §4.2). Isolate missing tool discovery, rule arithmetic, and post-block retries.
3. Sampled support Do multiple rollouts on suboptimal prompts explore distinct action paths? Eight-rollout action identity vs score headroom (§6.1). When rollouts repeat identical chains (121/197), vary sampling before rescoring.
4. Reward ordering Do unrounded returns strictly rank compliant chains over shortcuts? Component decomposition and tie inspection (§5.1, §6.1). Adjust component weights whenever partial ties trade off wrong attributes.
5. Post-norm advantage Does credit survive centering, scaling, discounting, and clipping? Zero-advantage step counts (136/230), KL vs PG loss, action-span checks. Account for constant cancellation, γt\gamma^tγt delay, and shrinkage overlap.
6. Updates and off-policy checks Do positive-advantage actions gain selection probability and hold off-policy? Per-step actor log-probs paired with (S, T, E, X) evals. Screen checkpoints early on held-out ordering metrics.

7.2. Limitations

Four scope boundaries govern these results. First, all SFT and RL experiments evaluate one 8B model (Qwen3-8B with thinking disabled and LoRA rank/alpha 32) on a single 80GB A800 GPU, leaving other parameter scales and architectures unmeasured. Second, Ask-or-Act uses deterministic simulated tools and Chinese request templates tailored to ordering and dependency shifts, and transient retries (P11) and multi-page exhaustion (P13) lack standalone fields in the nine-field evaluator. Third, single-run data support GRPO #2–#4, SFT-GRPO #2, historical Q-tables, and VPR. Multi-seed checks span three state-baseline seeds and two GiGPO seeds to establish seed sensitivity and visit collapse within those arms. Fourth, each WebShop page-text treatment restarts independently from the initial search page separately from any frozen pre-treatment prefix, so purchase counts combine full-episode protocol differences with repeated subpage visits.

7.3. Conclusion

Evaluating multi-step LLM agents on terminal success alone conceals regressions in pre-write information gathering, operation intent, and returned-identifier provenance across SFT, continued GRPO, and direct GRPO. On Ask-or-Act, commercial API models query the relocated policy tool before writing and diverge primarily on numerical comparison rules. Five fine-tuned Qwen3-8B checkpoints and five repair interventions frequently complete shifted tasks through post-block retries at 0/60 pre-write queries. In WebShop, process visit rewards lift visitation past 99% at unchanged task scores because the policy opens one subpage right before buying and skips alternative comparisons. Across 500 WebShop prompts, 197 groups receive zero GRPO reward contrast below full credit, with 121 repeating identical action sequences across eight rollouts.

Researchers auditing agent RL checkpoints should verify pre-write call order and identifier binding alongside completion rates. Intermediate tied groups also require checking action diversity before tuning reward weights, and training logs should pair zero-advantage step counts with active KL drift. Two open mechanistic questions merit step-level log-probability tracing. First, how early exploration locks in vs erases pre-write policy queries across state-baseline seeds before step 120 remains open while late rewards and advantages overlap. Second, future tracing can quantify how much VPR or historical-table credits shift conditional selection probabilities for rare required options after token-mean weighting and PPO clipping.

7.4. Availability of Materials

The public AskAct-EvalRL archive (AskAct-EvalRL) releases all Ask-or-Act task generators, deterministic evaluators, replay tools, reward estimators, verl training integrations, and collision scripts at commit f30851b894d9c52aab8a15edabf883ce4088fc78. Evaluations pair with WebShop commit 64fa2a5c15c7daa698b9ac93f5bb5437b634c9bd (WebShop snapshot) and verl v0.8.0. Running python -m askact.run_suite --agent rule in Python 3.12+ reproduces the 260-task original email rule benchmark offline on CPU with zero network requirement. We detail the repository modules, training hyperparameters, and bundle verification checks in Appendix A.
