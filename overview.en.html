Scores and Behavioral Evidence in Agent Reinforcement Learning

Author: Hao Fu

Abstract

A reinforcement learning run can eliminate premature writes on familiar tasks while dropping a required pre-write policy query from 60/60 to 0/60 at unchanged task success. We evaluate supervised fine-tuning (SFT), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) continued from SFT, and direct GRPO on Qwen3-8B LoRA across 1,780 Ask-or-Act tasks and WebShop. Continuing GRPO from a 192-demonstration SFT checkpoint raises Ask-or-Act training success from 178/192 to 192/192 and lowers premature writes on 20 paired email tasks from 19/20 to 0/20. Pre-write policy queries on 60 two-tool email tasks drop from 60/60 to 0/60, with the exact same 50/60 tasks succeeding through post-block retries. Direct GRPO keeps 58/60 pre-write queries, triggers premature actions on 60/60 two-tool tasks (28 solely from mismatched intents), and passes returned identifiers on 6/30 completed scheduling tasks. In WebShop, a subpage visit reward raises pre-purchase visitation from 94/500 to 498/499 valid runs and lowers subsequent competing-product clicks from 1,166/1,169 to 4/3,970 trajectories. Independent conflicting subpage-text treatments yield 0/198 changes in target-purchase Booleans, and 403/500 eight-rollout prompt groups tie in terminal return (197 below full credit, 121 with identical action sequences). These single-GPU 8B LoRA findings motivate a six-step diagnostic sequence separating evaluation coverage, sampled candidate support, reward ordering, and gradient delivery.

Keywords: LLM agents, reinforcement learning, GRPO, behavioral evaluation, reward shaping, credit assignment
