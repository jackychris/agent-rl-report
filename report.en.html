Scores and Behavioral Evidence in Agent Reinforcement Learning

Abstract

A reinforcement learning run can eliminate premature writes on familiar tasks while dropping a required pre-write policy query from 60/60 to 0/60 at unchanged task success. We evaluate supervised fine-tuning (SFT), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) continued from SFT, and direct GRPO on Qwen3-8B LoRA across 1,780 Ask-or-Act tasks and WebShop. Continuing GRPO from a 192-demonstration SFT checkpoint raises Ask-or-Act training success from 178/192 to 192/192 and lowers premature writes on 20 paired email tasks from 19/20 to 0/20. Pre-write policy queries on 60 two-tool email tasks drop from 60/60 to 0/60, with the exact same 50/60 tasks succeeding through post-block retries. Direct GRPO keeps 58/60 pre-write queries, triggers premature actions on 60/60 two-tool tasks (28 solely from mismatched intents), and passes returned identifiers on 6/30 completed scheduling tasks. In WebShop, a subpage visit reward raises pre-purchase visitation from 94/500 to 498/499 valid runs and lowers subsequent competing-product clicks from 1,166/1,169 to 4/3,970 trajectories. Independent conflicting subpage-text treatments yield 0/198 changes in target-purchase Booleans, and 403/500 eight-rollout prompt groups tie in terminal return (197 below full credit, 121 with identical action sequences). These single-GPU 8B LoRA findings motivate a six-step diagnostic sequence separating evaluation coverage, sampled candidate support, reward ordering, and gradient delivery.

Keywords: LLM agents, reinforcement learning, GRPO, behavioral evaluation, reward shaping, credit assignment

2. Background and Related Work

2.1. Outcome RL and Process Supervision for Tool-Using Agents

Outcome-based RL over multi-turn tool use raises final task accuracy in Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025) and ReTool (Feng et al., 2025a), applying PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) or GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to models such as Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025). That gain leaves open which intermediate strategies support the score. In terminal agents, Yao et al. (2026) show that SFT skill diversity governs downstream RL generalization even at matched post-SFT accuracy. They further show that rewarding pre-completion verification raises check frequency on its own and requires repetition penalties to lift task success, paralleling our WebShop and Ask-or-Act results.

Token-level loss with single-step updates causes outcome-reward GRPO to induce an implicit Monte-Carlo process reward over shared prefixes (Sullivan and Koller, 2025). Explicit shaping therefore helps when it supplies separation beyond that implicit credit. Classical potential-based shaping preserves optimal policies through γΦ(s′)−Φ(s)\gamma \Phi(s') - \Phi(s)γΦ(s′)−Φ(s) (Ng et al., 1999). Inside GRPO's within-group normalization, Wang (2026) shows that tied outcome returns cancel outcome contrast while scaling drives tiny shaping terms at full update strength, matching §6.1. Structured process methods curb shallow proxy gaming using workflow completeness checks in RLTR (Li et al., 2025) and dependency-ordered argument and budget scoring in PROVE (Abdelaziz et al., 2026). Related designs introduce learned progress critics in AgentPRM (Choudhury, 2025; Xi et al., 2025) or calibrated rubric critics in Kimi K2 (Kimi Team, 2025). Ask-or-Act holds the deterministic executor fixed and swaps tool locations and numeric rules at test time to isolate behavioral regressions (AskAct-EvalRL).

2.2. Tool Necessity and Evidence Use

Excessive or ungrounded tool calls appear in both efficiency and attribution studies. Zeng et al. (2026a) and OTC-PO (Wang et al., 2025b) show that outcome-only rewards tolerate redundant tool calls that joint outcome-efficiency objectives trim. Wu et al. (2026), ToolVision (Mao et al., 2026), and Search-G1 (Cheng et al., 2026) measure per-query tool necessity and answer reliance by toggling tool access or dropping retrieved snippets. SMART (Qian et al., 2025) trains explicit necessity rationales including user clarification turns. Both IGPO (Wang et al., 2025a) and TIPS (Xie et al., 2026) score intermediate turns by the ground-truth answer's log-probability gain under the current or frozen policy.

Several diagnostics test whether models use retrieved content as evidence. RAGChecker (Ru et al., 2024) separates retrieval coverage from faithfulness at claim level, and ToolFailBench (Soni, 2026) checks whether outputs respect mock-tool overrides of parametric priors. HERALD (Liu et al., 2026b) inserts counterfactual passages to expose citation hacking. Proof-of-Use (Ma et al., 2025) pairs normalized evidence identifiers with training ablations, and AttriGuard (He et al., 2026b) rewrites observations behind identical action prefixes to detect injection susceptibility. We construct paired (A, B) branch controls in Ask-or-Act N and evaluate whole-protocol differences across independent initial-page WebShop runs.

2.3. Zero-Variance Groups and Candidate Diversity in GRPO

Saturation on easy or hard prompts removes within-group variance. Zhou et al. (2026) formulate the GRPO policy gradient as a U-statistic to size group sampling. Both DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) and Query Recycling (Coelho et al., 2026) discard or defer zero-variance groups until policy updates restore score spread. AVSPO (He et al., 2026a) injects synthetic extreme returns to break ties while weighting real rollouts uniformly. CIGPO (Dou, 2026) adds turn-level likelihood gain when format penalties pin all rollouts to -2.0.

Our WebShop sample census in §6.1 divides intermediate tied prompt groups by candidate diversity. All eight rollouts execute the exact same environment-action sequence across 121 of the 197 tied groups below full credit, leaving gradient contrast at zero under rescoring. Branching rollout trees in PORTool (Wu et al., 2025), teacher recovery from failed states in OVCSD (Xia et al., 2026), and expert-step contrastive critiques in ACT (Liu et al., 2026a) target that missing candidate support.

2.4. Step-Level Credit, Token Weighting, and Rollout–Actor Alignment

Critic-free action-boundary methods include repeated-state grouping in GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025b) and RTMC (Wang et al., 2026b), closely related to our WebShop GiGPO and Ask-or-Act group-anchor baselines. ProGPO (Fan and Liu, 2026) compares exact-prefix peers and reports 33.7% WebShop and 44.5% ALFWorld steps lacking exact-prefix peers before transition credit. TRIAGE (Xu et al., 2026) assigns LLM step-role weights, CAPO (Wang et al., 2026a) trains boundary critics, and Agent Lightning (Luo et al., 2025) decouples agent execution from trajectory training.

Optimization aggregation and engine alignment also govern how advantages move parameters. DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) uses global token-mean loss aggregation, and GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) clips length-normalized sequence ratios. Balanced Aggregation (Zeng et al., 2026b) reweights unequal-length positive and negative rollouts, and Token Gradient Cancellation (Ding et al., 2026) removes weight drift across shared tokens. Tensor-parallel reduction variance (Zhang et al., 2025), quantization drift in QaRL (Gu et al., 2026), and rollout-vs-trainer log-probability divergence in VeXact (Zhong et al., 2026) match the token maxima and 39.5× sequence-to-token χ2\chi^2χ2 ratio we measure between SGLang rollouts and FSDP updates.

3. Controlled Behavioral Evaluation in Ask-or-Act

3.1. Interaction Modes, Counterfactual Pairs, and Five Domains

Static benchmarks and general text environments such as ALFWorld (Shridhar et al., 2020) rarely hold environment facts fixed while flipping one authorization or retrieval rule. LLM-as-judge evaluators also introduce repeat instability. Resubmitting 46 accepted synthetic trajectories to DeepSeek-v4-flash drops repeat acceptance to 13/46 and 12/46 (35/46 under DeepSeek-v4-pro). Ask-or-Act relies on single-condition counterfactual pairs and deterministic code replay (AskAct-EvalRL).

Across every task, the model chooses from 25 callable tools spanning six interaction modes (15 read queries, six writes, and four user or control actions), listed in Table 3.1.

3.2. Executor Fact Ledger, Evaluation Fields, and Baseline Reward

Ask-or-Act keeps an internal executor fact ledger separate from tool observations returned to the model. Clarifying a file identifier updates that ledger with the file's sharing rule so the executor enforces preconditions even when retrieve_files remains uncalled. Every parsed tool call sent to the environment counts as an attempt, leaving state unchanged on blocked calls while recording violations and premature actions.

Policies that avoid required writes incur zero violations under naive negative metrics. On the 260 original email tasks at greedy decoding (T=0T=0T=0), a collapsed sft-targeted192 checkpoint executes retrieve_outbox 16 times followed by finish on every task. That checkpoint logs 0/260 policy_violation and 0/260 premature_action alongside 10/260 (3.8%) task success. Low required-write attempt rates also appear in tgt-payments (23.4%) and cln-scheduling (14.1%). Compliance counts therefore require explicit write denominators and failed_to_act.

For every trajectory, askact/evaluator.py deterministically computes nine behavior fields plus signature_match:

  • task_success, side_effect_count, and failed_to_act: terminal resolution, unintended writes, and omitted required writes.
  • policy_violation, confirmation_correctness, premature_action, and evidence_sufficient_at_write: action-condition checks against the executor ledger (null if no write attempt occurs).
  • necessary_clarification_recall, clarification_precision, and unnecessary_question_count: accuracy and redundancy of user clarifications.
  • signature_match: whether clarified fields, confirmation occurrence, and terminal state match the oracle tuple, intentionally excluding ordinary read order and handle provenance.

Completion frequently diverges from signature match. For instance, Grok-4.3 and Gemini-3.6-flash both solve 246/260 tasks while matching 169 and 101 signatures.

4.3. Five Repair Interventions

7.2. Limitations

Four scope boundaries govern these results. First, all SFT and RL experiments evaluate one 8B model (Qwen3-8B with thinking disabled and LoRA rank/alpha 32) on a single 80GB A800 GPU, leaving other parameter scales and architectures unmeasured. Second, Ask-or-Act uses deterministic simulated tools and Chinese request templates tailored to ordering and dependency shifts, and transient retries (P11) and multi-page exhaustion (P13) lack standalone fields in the nine-field evaluator. Third, single-run data support GRPO #2–#4, SFT-GRPO #2, historical Q-tables, and VPR. Multi-seed checks span three state-baseline seeds and two GiGPO seeds to establish seed sensitivity and visit collapse within those arms. Fourth, each WebShop page-text treatment restarts independently from the initial search page separately from any frozen pre-treatment prefix, so purchase counts combine full-episode protocol differences with repeated subpage visits.

7.3. Conclusion

Evaluating multi-step LLM agents on terminal success alone conceals regressions in pre-write information gathering, operation intent, and returned-identifier provenance across SFT, continued GRPO, and direct GRPO. On Ask-or-Act, commercial API models query the relocated policy tool before writing and diverge primarily on numerical comparison rules. Five fine-tuned Qwen3-8B checkpoints and five repair interventions frequently complete shifted tasks through post-block retries at 0/60 pre-write queries. In WebShop, process visit rewards lift visitation past 99% at unchanged task scores because the policy opens one subpage right before buying and skips alternative comparisons. Across 500 WebShop prompts, 197 groups receive zero GRPO reward contrast below full credit, with 121 repeating identical action sequences across eight rollouts.

Researchers auditing agent RL checkpoints should verify pre-write call order and identifier binding alongside completion rates. Intermediate tied groups also require checking action diversity before tuning reward weights, and training logs should pair zero-advantage step counts with active KL drift. Two open mechanistic questions merit step-level log-probability tracing. First, how early exploration locks in vs erases pre-write policy queries across state-baseline seeds before step 120 remains open while late rewards and advantages overlap. Second, future tracing can quantify how much VPR or historical-table credits shift conditional selection probabilities for rare required options after token-mean weighting and PPO clipping.
